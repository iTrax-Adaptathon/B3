How it works
One flight is late. AeroMind works out everything else that breaks — and fixes it before it spreads.
1
A delay comes in
An aircraft is going to land forty minutes late. Its new arrival and departure times are worked out straight away.
2
AeroMind finds every conflict
It checks the gate the aircraft was meant to use, whether the crew has had enough rest, and whether any bag on board can still make its connecting flight.
3
It fixes them automatically
A new gate, a new crew, bags sent to the right belt or re-booked onto a later flight. If nothing is free, it moves a lower-priority flight out of the way and then solves that flight's problems too.
4
It checks its own work
Before anything is saved, AeroMind proves no two flights share a gate or a crew. If that promise would break, the whole plan is thrown away. Every decision is written down in plain English.

Where this came from
What we kept, and what we added.

The original operations dashboard is all still here: Live Flight Operations, Operational Impact, the Autonomous Decision feed, Live Alerts and Resource Status — along with the engines behind them for spotting conflicts, tracing knock-on impact and reassigning resources.

AeroMind extends that foundation in five ways. It respects real turnaround and rest gaps instead of just comparing times. It resolves knock-on changes several flights deep, moving a lower-priority flight when nothing else is free. It lets you preview a plan before committing to it. It re-books baggage that would otherwise miss a connection. And it guarantees, after every single change, that no two flights share a gate or a crew — rolling the whole plan back if that promise cannot be kept.
